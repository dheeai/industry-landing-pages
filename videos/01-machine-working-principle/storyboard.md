# Sample 01 — Machine working-principle explainer

**Segment:** Machinery  
**Runtime:** 60 seconds  
**Status:** Storyboard, voice-over and shot sequence approved by the user for a fictional concept sample.
**Portfolio disclosure:** Fictional, unbranded machine. AI-assisted concept; not a representation of a real product or its performance.  
**Format:** 16:9 master; captions and titles composited in post.  
**Visual method:** Establish a consistent fictional machine reference, develop a simple 3D/exploded process view where available, generate restrained motion shots, then add all text, arrows and DHEE branding deterministically in post. No generated logos or embedded text.

## Story

**Setup:** A clean, unbranded industrial cell looks complicated when viewed as a single object.  
**Turn:** Follow one generic workpiece through the cell to make the sequence legible.  
**Ending:** Return to the full machine; its role is now easier to explain because the viewer can see input, process and output.

## Voice-over and timed storyboard

| Time | Picture and motion | Voice-over | On-screen text (added in post) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00–06 | A single, unmarked workpiece rests at the start of a quiet conveyor. The fictional machine sits beyond it, clean and still. Slow push toward the infeed. | “A machine is easier to understand when you can follow the work.” | `FOLLOW THE WORK` |
| 06–13 | The workpiece moves into a compact, fictional modular cell. Keep the geometry stable and the action simple. | “Start with one part entering the line.” | `INPUT` |
| 13–21 | The machine is shown as a restrained cutaway: three clear zones inside one enclosure. A single path connects them. | “Inside, a sequence of stages turns a complicated system into a process you can see.” | `ONE CONNECTED PROCESS` |
| 21–30 | The workpiece moves through the middle zone. Use abstract mechanical movement and a simple highlight; avoid implying a specific operation, speed or safety procedure. | “The important story is not a list of components. It is how the work moves from one stage to the next.” | `PROCESS` |
| 30–39 | The piece leaves the cell at the outfeed. The path resolves into three labeled points, added in post. | “What comes in. What happens in between. What comes out.” | `INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT` |
| 39–50 | Pull back to the whole cell in a neutral factory setting. A fine line traces the piece's route once, then fades. | “When customers can follow that sequence, they have a clearer way to understand where the machine fits—and what to ask next.” | `SEE WHERE IT FITS` |
| 50–60 | Minimal end frame: the fictional machine in profile, then a clean DHEE title card. | “DHEE helps industrial teams make complex products easier to understand. Start with one machine and one clear story.” | `MAKE COMPLEX MACHINERY EASIER TO UNDERSTAND` / `DHEE` |

## Generation and edit notes

- Keep one unbranded fictional machine design consistent across all generated shots; no real company logos, product names or performance claims.
- Avoid precise controls, guards, safety instructions, operating steps, machine specifications and certification marks.
- Use captions for the complete voice-over. If voice-over is removed, keep the same meaning in concise on-screen captions.
- Composite labels, arrows, timing, captions and DHEE mark in the deterministic edit; do not ask the image/video model to render text.
- Create a poster from the approved opening or whole-machine frame. Make 9:16 and 1:1 edits only after the 16:9 master is reviewed.
- Add AI workflow model/version/licence details to the model register before any external or commercial use.
